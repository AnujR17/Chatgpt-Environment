# Checkpoint — Nori reflection and CMA reports

Nori replaces Pip. Reflection is the default: a longer observation window and stable holds help reflect sustained visual patterns. A brief grin or grimace no longer switches the companion immediately. Your confirmed check-in takes priority and is labelled with its age. These are reflections to interpret, not a validated reading of felt mood.

Face preview remains available for instant camera/video tests. Mouth widening, pressed lips, smile support, jaw, independent eyes, brows, upper-lip/nose movement, visible teeth and hand proximity are separated. Possible hand-over-mouth occlusion withholds hidden mouth/category cues. All 43 check-in words now have distinct profiles; the categorical model remains eight classes with its existing accuracy limits. No 43-class emotion detector is claimed.

The native and phone reports now export a CMA PDF: pleasantness/activation journeys, first/latest and most activated check-ins, sample region counts and a useful reflection prompt. Face tracking does not fabricate check-in values or an emotional duration log. Hold Ctrl+Alt and drag Nori, or choose Move Nori from the tray; release locks it and remembers its position.

If using 0.3, choose Settings → Check for updates → Update and restart. The new hand task travels in the app payload; the Python/runtime and existing face models are unchanged. A full portable ZIP is also available for fresh installs. Sources remain off after restart. Tracking is local and no camera/game images are uploaded.

Provider-model loading, hand-example detection, recorded-face mapping, synthetic movement/occlusion/render tests, sustained reflection, PDF/browser integration and Windows restart/rollback checks are separate evidence. Physical hand-to-mouth/teeth accuracy, subjective usefulness and an actual update on the user's PC still need target-context testing. See NORI-REFLECTION-AND-CMA.md.
