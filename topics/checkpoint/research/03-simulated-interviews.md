# Synthetic interview rehearsals

Prepared 2 November 2026 · ALL RESPONSES BELOW ARE INVENTED

These are hypothetical answers from the five fictional personas, written to rehearse the questionnaire and expose assumptions. They are not interviews, survey results, quotations from public posters, predictions validated by research or evidence that anyone wants the product. No one was contacted. Do not put these answers in the primary-research sheets or present them as five participants in coursework.

The shared questions I01–I12 appear in the interview guide. The same questions are answered for all five personas. The factual evidence behind each persona is linked in the research report; the invented answers below are not attributed to those sources.

## P1. Rohan · Indian competitive player · Synthetic answers

| Question | Hypothetical answer, written by AI |
| --- | --- |
| I01 Recent session and goal | I wanted a couple of competitive matches and something to improve at. A loss would have been okay if we communicated and I learned something. |
| I02 Less enjoyable moment | We lost several rounds and a teammate started making it personal. I kept replaying what they said instead of focusing on the next round. |
| I03 Why it mattered | I can accept playing badly. I find it harder when strangers decide a mistake says something about me as a person. |
| I04 Enjoyable difficulty | A close match with friends can feel good even when we lose. The same score feels different when people encourage each other. |
| I05 What changed | I might answer back, rush decisions or become quiet. Fast mouse movement alone would not tell you which of those is happening. |
| I06 Continue or stop | I sometimes want to queue again to end differently. I would need help noticing whether I actually want another match, rather than a rule deciding for me. |
| I07 Existing coping | Playing with trusted friends and muting a specific person can help. Being told to relax during an argument would probably annoy me. |
| I08 Suitable timing | After the match is much better. Even when I am dead, I may still need to watch and communicate. |
| I09 Concept preference | I would try a quick results-screen choice. I would reject a breathing screen that covers the game or makes me abandon a round. |
| I10 Data boundaries | Start with match events and my own check-in. I do not yet see why this needs my face, my whole desktop or a recording of our call. |
| I11 Misreading | Let me say I am enjoying the competition. Do not make me correct the same assumption every few minutes. |
| I12 Keep or remove | I would keep it if it helps me make a better decision about continuing. I would remove it if it lowers performance or talks down to me. |

**Unvalidated assumptions to test:** That this segment wants post-match support, sometimes continues to repair a bad ending, and would prefer a check-in over sensing. None of these preferences has been established by the public anchor account.

**Useful interview revision:** Ask for an actual requeue incident rather than assuming loss chasing. Probe whether post-match support still feels intrusive.

## P2. Kavya · Indian multilingual competitor · Synthetic answers

| Question | Hypothetical answer, written by AI |
| --- | --- |
| I01 Recent session and goal | I wanted to play with people who would communicate enough to make the match interesting. I wanted to participate, not spend the session negotiating how I should sound. |
| I02 Less enjoyable moment | After I spoke, someone started commenting on my gender and language. I stopped giving useful calls because I did not want to invite more attention. |
| I03 Why it mattered | The obstacle was their behaviour. I do not want a system to interpret the situation as me failing to manage my emotions. |
| I04 Enjoyable difficulty | I can enjoy a hard game with respectful teammates. Being challenged by an opponent and being targeted by a teammate are different experiences. |
| I05 What changed | I may speak less while still feeling upset. A microphone might treat the silence as improvement even though the experience became worse. |
| I06 Continue or stop | I might mute one person, continue with the rest or finish the session. I want those to remain my decisions. |
| I07 Existing coping | Trusted groups and specific mute/report controls are useful possibilities. Muting everyone loses communication I might still want. |
| I08 Suitable timing | Help finding a control should be available when I ask. A reflection question can wait until the match is over. |
| I09 Concept preference | I prefer support dealing with unwanted communication. A generic calm-down message would feel like the product had misunderstood the problem. |
| I10 Data boundaries | I would want clear boundaries around other people's voices and my room. I would not assume a speech model understands every language or joke correctly. |
| I11 Misreading | Let me identify the cause in my own words. Do not publish a mood label or infer that a language I use makes me aggressive. |
| I12 Keep or remove | I would keep it if it preserves participation and control. I would reject it if it blames me, hides all communication or submits reports without asking. |

**Unvalidated assumptions to test:** Preference for particular control access, willingness to discuss incidents, and attitudes to sensing. A person who has faced harassment may choose a completely different response or want no tool.

**Useful interview revision:** Ask what support felt respectful in the person's actual experience; do not ask them to agree that mute/report is the answer.

## P3. Aman · Indian player with limited time · Synthetic answers

| Question | Hypothetical answer, written by AI |
| --- | --- |
| I01 Recent session and goal | I wanted a short enjoyable break after a demanding day. I did not necessarily want to achieve something or improve a rank. |
| I02 Less enjoyable moment | I spent much of the available time deciding what to play and worrying that I would not make meaningful progress. |
| I03 Why it mattered | I finally had access to the games I wanted, but not the energy or time I imagined having. Scheduling fun made it feel like another assignment. |
| I04 Enjoyable difficulty | A short difficult section can be satisfying when I have time for it. The same section feels different when I need to stop soon. |
| I05 What changed | I may become indecisive, browse menus or stop quietly. I am not necessarily angry, and a bored-looking face would not explain the situation. |
| I06 Continue or stop | I sometimes close the game rather than force myself. A good stopping point could help, but another compulsory task would not. |
| I07 Existing coping | Shorter activities might fit better on some days. Advice that treats finishing more games as the goal would miss what I need. |
| I08 Suitable timing | Before starting or at a natural stopping point. Do not ask me to spend ten minutes setting up a tool for a twenty-minute session. |
| I09 Concept preference | I might try a simple time or intention check. I might also prefer no intervention if the game is already helping me unwind. |
| I10 Data boundaries | A timer and voluntary check-in seem sufficient initially. I would need a compelling reason to turn on continuous camera or audio analysis. |
| I11 Misreading | Ask whether I am still getting what I wanted from the session. Do not call me disengaged just because I am playing slowly. |
| I12 Keep or remove | I would keep something that makes leisure easier. I would remove streaks, completion targets or repeated notifications that make it feel like productivity tracking. |

**Unvalidated assumptions to test:** That a session intention or stopping cue helps instead of adding pressure. The original account establishes time and energy tensions, not acceptance of this design.

**Useful interview revision:** Offer a no-tool alternative and ask whether planning itself is part of the problem.

## P4. Alex · Challenge-oriented player · Synthetic answers

| Question | Hypothetical answer, written by AI |
| --- | --- |
| I01 Recent session and goal | I wanted to master a difficult section. Part of the satisfaction is working out an approach that feels like mine. |
| I02 Less enjoyable moment | I lost a lot of progress after finally getting past a difficult point. For a moment it felt like I had learned nothing. |
| I03 Why it mattered | The loss itself was not the only issue. I could not tell what to change, so repeating the attempt stopped feeling like practice. |
| I04 Enjoyable difficulty | I enjoy repeating an attempt when I can see improvement. I might swear and laugh even when I want to keep going. |
| I05 What changed | Attempts might become rushed or I might pause to think. The system would need to distinguish experimenting from acting without a plan. |
| I06 Continue or stop | I may try a slower method, look for an explanation or leave it for another day. A break can be a choice without implying defeat. |
| I07 Existing coping | Community advice sometimes helps, but unsolicited spoilers can remove the reason I was playing. I want control over the level of help. |
| I08 Suitable timing | After I have recovered from a fall or opened a menu. Not while I am trying a delicate movement or immediately after every mistake. |
| I09 Concept preference | An unobtrusive optional-help button sounds plausible. Automatically making the game easier would spoil the achievement for me. |
| I10 Data boundaries | I could consider sharing the game window. I would not assume that a camera tells the system more than asking whether I still want the challenge. |
| I11 Misreading | Let me mark the struggle as wanted and silence further prompts for a while. Do not turn that into permission to encourage endless attempts. |
| I12 Keep or remove | I would keep it if it respects discovery and helps when asked. I would remove it if it treats perseverance as a problem or gives away a solution. |

**Unvalidated assumptions to test:** Preference for no spoilers, willingness to share a window, and the interpretation of rushing. Public community accounts do not establish universal challenge-player preferences. One evidence anchor is mobile, so desktop transfer is also a hypothesis.

**Useful interview revision:** Compare two similar falls and ask what made one acceptable and the other unwanted.

## P5. Morgan · Player and streamer · Synthetic answers

| Question | Hypothetical answer, written by AI |
| --- | --- |
| I01 Recent session and goal | I wanted both an enjoyable game and a stream people would want to watch. Those goals can overlap, but they are not identical. |
| I02 Less enjoyable moment | A difficult section coincided with chat criticism. It became hard to tell whether I was reacting for the show or continuing something I no longer enjoyed. |
| I03 Why it mattered | Stopping can feel different when other people are watching. At the same time, I do not want a tool making assumptions about my audience or my income. |
| I04 Enjoyable difficulty | A dramatic reaction can be part of the fun. The audience sees a performance and cannot always know how I feel. |
| I05 What changed | I might keep smiling or exaggerate a complaint. Visible expression alone would be an unreliable account of whether I want support. |
| I06 Continue or stop | I might call a break, change activities or ask a moderator for help. What I choose depends on the situation, not a fixed anger score. |
| I07 Existing coping | An agreed break cue might help. Publicly labelling my mood could give chat a new thing to focus on. |
| I08 Suitable timing | A private surface and a moment I can choose. A prompt that appears on the broadcast changes the interaction even if its wording is gentle. |
| I09 Concept preference | I would consider a private manual check-in before automatic facial inference. I would want to preview exactly what viewers can see. |
| I10 Data boundaries | Camera consent for broadcasting is not automatically consent for emotion analysis. I would need separate controls and clarity about audience visibility. |
| I11 Misreading | Let me say it was performance, genuine frustration or both. Do not make that label visible to viewers or turn it into a personality profile. |
| I12 Keep or remove | I would keep it if it fits my chosen workflow. I would remove it after unwanted disclosure or repeated incorrect interpretations. |

**Unvalidated assumptions to test:** All proposed preferences, including the usefulness of a private check-in. Published research establishes emotional-labour issues in its sample, not these exact answers or their generality to all streamers.

**Useful interview revision:** Ask the participant to identify actual broadcast surfaces and review a mock prompt there, rather than assuming “private” means a second monitor.

## What the rehearsal changes

These are research-planning consequences, not findings about players:

- Keep the question about enjoyable difficulty; otherwise the study is biased towards equating challenge with harm.
- Ask about the cause in the participant's words before offering categories.
- Include quiet dissatisfaction, unwanted communication and technical issues.
- Ask when help would be harmful, not just whether help sounds useful.
- Present no-tool and manual-support alternatives alongside automatic sensing.
- Test data sources separately; acceptance of one is not consent to all.
- Ask about correction and stopping the system before discussing personalisation.
- Keep all predictions replaceable. If real participants reject them, update the design.
