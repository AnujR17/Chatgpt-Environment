# Primary research kit: five real participants

Prepared 2 November 2026 · Status: study proposal, not conducted

## Purpose

Understand when challenge becomes unwanted, what causes that change, what players already do, and whether a context-aware support tool would be useful. Explore the need before testing whether the proposed system is desirable.

Do not read the simulated answers before an actual interview if doing so is likely to bias your questioning. Keep them out of the primary-research dataset.

## Recruitment targets

Recruit five adults, one for each context below. These are purposive contrasts, not a representative sample. Do not search for people who match the fictional personas perfectly.

| Slot | Target context | Important contrast |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | Indian ranked team-game player | An enjoyable loss versus an unwanted continuation |
| R2 | Indian multiplayer player with experience of unwanted communication; seek a woman and/or multilingual player willing to discuss it | Social harm versus ordinary performance frustration |
| R3 | Indian player with limited leisure time or energy | Dissatisfaction without overt rage |
| R4 | Player of a difficult single-player game | Chosen persistence versus wanting assistance |
| R5 | Adult who streams gameplay | Public expression versus private preference for help |

Participants should be able to decline questions, optional recording or optional sensing. A person who refuses camera access remains an informative participant. Do not replace them merely because they question the concept.

## Screener

S1. Are you 18 or older? Yes / No. Use this adult pilot only for respondents answering Yes.

S2. In what country do you usually play, and which game servers do you usually use? You may leave this blank.

S3. What games and modes have you played in the last month? Which devices do you use?

S4. What usually brings you to those games: relaxation, improvement, competition, friends, creating content, or another reason? Select more than one if needed.

S5. Have you recently experienced a session that became less enjoyable than you wanted? Yes / No / Unsure. Also recruit a contrasting positive experience; this is not an eligibility test for anger.

S6. Which languages do you prefer for the interview and for gaming? Do you stream gameplay? Would you be comfortable discussing unwanted communication, or prefer to skip it?

Ask about availability and any accessibility needs separately. Avoid collecting usernames, rank history, contact details or other identifying information unless necessary for scheduling or the study objective. Keep scheduling information outside the research sheet.

## Opening script

“I am exploring how gaming support could respond to difficult or unsatisfying moments. I am studying the design, not judging your skill or emotional control. You can skip any question or stop. I would like to take de-identified notes. Recording, screen sharing, camera and microphone analysis are separate optional choices. Nothing will be posted publicly without your permission. Please avoid sharing other players' identifying information. Is that acceptable, and which optional activities, if any, are you comfortable with?”

Before recording, state the actual storage location, who can access it, the agreed deletion date and the withdrawal contact/process. Fill these in honestly; do not promise “local only” if using a cloud recording service. Agree separately whether anonymised quotations can appear in coursework. No automatic uploads or reports to a game publisher.

## Twelve core questions

Use the same core questions across participants. Ask open questions first; use examples only if needed. Do not assume the participant rages, wants AI or accepts cameras.

| ID | Question | Optional neutral probes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I01 | Tell me about your most recent gaming session. What did you want from it? | What game/mode, alone or with others, how much time, and how did you feel before starting? |
| I02 | Describe a specific recent moment when the session became less enjoyable than you wanted. What happened immediately before and after it? | Walk through the sequence. What did you notice first? It is fine if no such moment comes to mind. |
| I03 | What made that moment matter to you? | Was it progress, control, another person, technical performance, available time, or something else? Do not assume the cause from the game event. |
| I04 | Describe a difficult moment that you still enjoyed. How was it different? | Could an observer tell? What would they get wrong? |
| I05 | During the less enjoyable moment, what changed in your thoughts, actions or communication, if anything? | What was visible? Did you go quiet? Could a camera or microphone miss it or misinterpret it? |
| I06 | What did you do next, and how did you decide whether to continue? | What helped, made no difference or made it worse? What happened later in the session? |
| I07 | What have you tried before to handle similar situations? | Ask for a real example and an approach that did not work. Include friends, settings, practice or stopping if the person brings them up. |
| I08 | When, if ever, could an offer of help fit into your play? | Ask for the worst possible time too. Is a round transition actually free time? Would you prefer to request help? |
| I09 | Now that you have seen these concept options, which would you use, change or reject, and why? | Compare a results-screen check-in, optional technique help, access to mute/report instructions, and a simple manual button. Include “none.” Rotate presentation order. |
| I10 | What information, if any, would you let this tool use? What conditions would matter? | Ask separately about selected game-window capture, input timing, camera, microphone features, transcripts and session history. What should happen when you say no? |
| I11 | Imagine the tool misreads you. What should it say or do, and how would you correct it? | Show “Still enjoying this?” with Continue / Optional help / Break / Turn off. Does it interrupt? What should it remember or forget? |
| I12 | What would make you keep using this, and what would make you remove it? | What outcome matters more than winning? What problem have we misunderstood? Is no tool preferable? |

## Context-specific follow-ups

For R1: Ask about a loss they accepted and a win that still felt bad. Explore requeue decisions without asserting that they chase losses. What would interfere with team responsibilities?

For R2: Ask whether the issue was ordinary disagreement, exclusion, harassment or something else, in the participant's words. What support would preserve wanted communication? What language/cultural context would automated interpretation miss? Do not ask them to reproduce slurs or confront an abuser.

For R3: Ask how a short session fits around the rest of the day. Does planning help or add pressure? What does a satisfying stopping point look like?

For R4: Ask whether advice would spoil discovery or change the meaning of an achievement. Would a recent setback still be acceptable if repeated? How do they ask for help today?

For R5: Ask whether the audience changes what they show. Which surfaces are broadcast? What support could appear privately? What would they not want a system to infer?

## Proposed 45–60-minute session

1. Consent and context, approximately 5 minutes.
2. Incident interview, I01–I08, approximately 20 minutes. Avoid revealing the full concept before hearing existing behaviour.
3. Concept comparison, I09–I12, approximately 10 minutes. Use identical materials and rotate the order of alternatives.
4. Optional familiar-game observation or participant-supplied clip review, approximately 10–15 minutes. Participant chooses a comfortable activity. Do not provoke anger or expose them to harassment.
5. Debrief, approximately 5 minutes. Ask what the study misunderstood and confirm retention/withdrawal preferences.

The timings are planning estimates. If the observation is omitted, retain the interview; do not invent observed behaviour from what the participant describes.

## Observation sheet

Record an anonymous participant code, game/build/mode, device, session goal, consented inputs and whether the researcher or a model produced a label. Keep these distinct:

| Field | What belongs here |
| --- | --- |
| Timestamp | When the event occurred, with one agreed clock |
| Observable event | For example, a visible fall or results screen; include uncertainty |
| Player report | What the participant actually said; distinguish direct quotation from paraphrase |
| Valence | 1 = very unpleasant, 3 = neutral, 5 = very pleasant; optional, exploratory item |
| Arousal | 1 = very low activation, 3 = moderate, 5 = very high activation; optional |
| Wanted continuation | Yes / No / Unsure, asked directly rather than inferred |
| Cause in player's words | Do not replace it with the system's guess |
| System estimate | Value/label, method and uncertainty; do not call it ground truth |
| Intervention | What was shown, where and when; or None |
| Response | Used / dismissed / ignored / disabled, without treating these as emotional labels |
| Helpfulness and interruption | Ask separately, using 1–5 ratings and an explanation |
| Researcher interpretation | Explicitly marked interpretation, including alternatives |

Use ratings at agreed natural pauses or immediately after a short segment. A session-end rating cannot label every preceding second. These simple items are not represented as validated clinical or psychometric scales. Frequent questions can alter the experience; log that burden.

## Synthesis procedure

1. Transcribe or expand notes promptly, following consent. Preserve contradictions.
2. Create one factual incident timeline per participant before combining themes.
3. Code goals, obstacles, interpretations, visible behaviour, coping attempts, outcomes, acceptable timing and data boundaries. Allow new codes.
4. For each proposed insight, retain supporting and contradicting cases, exact evidence locations and your interpretation.
5. Update or discard the provisional personas. Real participants may span several profiles or fit none.
6. Choose a direction using observed needs, not agreement with synthetic answers.

Do not report simulated answers as participants. Do not claim “80% of gamers” from four of five interviewees. You may say “four participants in this exploratory sample reported X,” while also describing the exception and recruitment limits.

## Testing a later prototype

First test the event detector on annotated recordings from the exact supported game/build. Report per-event precision and recall, timing delay and failure cases. Keep unsupported footage as unknown rather than forcing a label.

Then test affect estimates against nearby self-reports. Compare context-only, context-plus-check-in and optional added sensing. With a larger dataset, split by person so the same person's frames do not leak across training and testing. Five interviews cannot establish generalisable detection accuracy.

Finally test intervention value. Compare player-requested support, context-triggered support and no prompt where feasible; counterbalance order and acknowledge changes in skill, fatigue and game difficulty. Do not claim a five-person pilot proves effectiveness. Ask about control, enjoyment, usefulness and interruption separately from win/loss.
