# RBI Payments Vision 2028 — Summary
*Digital Cheque project · written 2026-09-26 · read from the official RBI PDF (all 17 pages)*

Source: [Payments Vision 2028, official PDF](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs//PublicationReport/Pdfs/PAYMENTSVISION2028270326500316AFADEB47259CB970132BA01304.PDF)

**Most important point:** the whole cheque section is **two sentences long**. The RBI commits only to *explore* e-cheques. There is no design, timeline, legal route or definition. So the question of what a digital cheque should be is completely open.

## The document at a glance
- **Issued by:** RBI, Department of Payment and Settlement Systems.
- **Dated:** 27 March 2026.
- **Covers:** up to 31 December 2028. The RBI can add initiatives that aren't listed.
- **Theme:** "Shaping India's Payment Frontier".
- **Main goal:** the challenge is "no longer one of expansion of reach alone, but of deepening trust" in payment systems, making them more resilient and expanding their global reach.
- **Focus areas:** user empowerment, fraud safeguards, cross-border efficiency, ease of doing business, and an "AI-led, data-driven approach".
- **Structure:** Preface, then Section 1 (history of past Vision documents), Section 2 (summary of this Vision), Section 3 (15 specific initiatives).

## The cheque section, in full (section 3.10)
- **Title:** "Review of design and security features of cheques and explore introduction of electronic cheques".
- **Problem stated:** cheques follow the CTS-2010 standard, but banks have each added their own extra security features, so cheques now vary across the system.
- **Action 1:** a full review of cheque design and security features, to make them uniform, strengthen fraud prevention and adopt best practices across all cheque instruments.
- **Action 2 (verbatim):** "cheques as a payment instrument offer some unique benefits over other payment methods. To leverage the unique benefits of paper-based instruments and the speed and reliability of electronic payments, and cater to new business use cases, introduction of electronic cheques in India shall be explored."
- **Not mentioned anywhere:** a timeline, a design, the NI Act, dishonour or s.138, clearing, signatures, or which "unique benefits" they mean.

## What this means for the project
- **We're working ahead of the regulator, not against it.** Our research can fill exactly what 3.10 leaves undefined: which benefits matter and for whom.
- **"Paper benefits + electronic speed"** is the RBI's own framing. It lines up with our design principle "formal, not frictional".
- **"New business use cases"** supports making MSMEs the primary segment.
- **3.1, 3.5, 3.13 and 3.15** give real regulatory precedents we can cite for payer control, shared accountability, a single status view and verified business identity.
