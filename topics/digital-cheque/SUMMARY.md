# Explanation and review of the uploaded archive

## Purpose

Digital Cheque explores how to make a future payment commitment usable without paper: issue it, hold it, track it, present it and handle a failure. It is a service system with payer, payee, bank and legal roles. MSME buyer-supplier relationships are the primary segment in the latest handoff; tenant-landlord relationships are secondary. Those priorities are historical project choices, not conclusions from interviews conducted here.

The core hypothesis is that a cheque's assurance matters more than the paper. The proposal seeks to retain that assurance while improving shared status, reminders, purpose records, verification and failure handling. This differs from simply replacing a cheque with an instant transfer.

## What is already available

- Foundation research into the problem, current lifecycle, stakeholders and existing payment alternatives.
- A broad list of legal, security, identity, settlement, records and UX factors.
- International precedent analysis, particularly Singapore EDP/EDP+ and Hong Kong e-Cheque.
- Nine scenario diagrams covering supplier payments, reserved funds, insufficient funds, unclaimed commitments, cancellation, legacy post-dated cheques, account tracks and disputes.
- Secondary synthesis exports: affinity clusters, certainties/suppositions/doubts, stakeholders, service blueprint and an MSME journey.
- A target 13-stage portfolio structure and historical progress/decision logs.

The handoff reports literature review, coding of 45 judgments, six synthetic artefacts and an expert interview guide as completed. Their full deliverables are not in the ZIP. External links are recorded, but their contents have not been inspected in this review. The five CSVs contain synthesis rather than a complete underlying evidence corpus.

## What is still unvalidated

The user confirms no interviews yet. Claims such as “payees drive demand,” “legal deterrence matters more than paper,” and “shared status reduces disputes” remain hypotheses. Synthetic artefacts are illustrative inputs, not observed behaviour. A board labelled as secondary synthesis should remain provisional until supported by traceable evidence and, where required, research.

The combined India hybrid is a concept. The archive does not prove that an Indian bank could lawfully issue it with all the proposed features. Current official sources and advocate/banker review are needed for instrument identity, signature standard, post-dating, cancellation, expiry, notice and evidence rules.

## Specific issues to resolve

| Issue | Finding and next action |
| --- | --- |
| Duplicate session logs | The shorter-named log says to discard an older pendency figure; the named Singapore log adds a later correction. Use the corrected log as the current historical reference while retaining both originals. |
| Statistical dates | The later sources report 35.16 lakh pending cases for December 2019 and 43 lakh for December 2024. They describe different dates; independently verify the underlying sources before publication. |
| Legal identity of reserved funds | The chosen hybrid is described as retaining cheque law, but another scenario implies reduced legal protection for its reserved-funds variant. Reservation alone does not establish this consequence. Define the legal route per variant. |
| Dispute after successful payment | Separate cheque dishonour from a later goods/services dispute. Do not treat the dishonour path as a general remedy after successful payment. |
| Expiry | The draft mixes Singapore's six-month term with the archive's three-month Indian cheque validity. Establish distinct, supported rules for validity, reservation and refunds. |
| Cancellation | The PDF's broad cancellation description differs from later bank-specific restrictions. Record source, bank, date and which variant each rule concerns. |
| Scope and timeline | The ideal 16-week plan differs from the later 12-day sprint. The ideal plan includes methods later recorded as cancelled. Set a current achievable scope rather than counting planned methods as executed. |
| India account tracks | A corporate/retail split inspired by Singapore is presented as resolved for India. Treat it as a proposed policy/product choice until supported by Indian rules. |
| Stakeholder count | One log describes four additions to nine stakeholders but lists five. Reconcile the actual roles before drawing a definitive map. |
| Legal countdowns | A single approximate total period conceals separate event-triggered deadlines. Verify each timer's trigger and exceptions before designing automatic notices or filing assistance. |
| Unsupported amendment | The archive itself retracts or questions a supposed mandatory mediation / section 138A change. Exclude it from requirements unless primary evidence establishes it. |
| Search limits | “No India model found” means the earlier search found none, not that none can exist. Turkey adoption numbers and some country comparisons remain incomplete. |

## Recommended order of work

1. Inventory the supplied and missing deliverables and build a claim register with source, date and verification status.
2. Agree the current segment, prototype scope and evidence requirements, using the historical plan as context.
3. Define instrument rules per variant: identity, issuance, acceptance, funds reservation, cancellation, presentment, expiry, payment failure and dispute.
4. Obtain and document appropriate user and expert evidence; update synthesis without inventing findings.
5. Produce the service state model, then prototype and test the key flows.
6. Write the case study with clear limits on what was researched, simulated and tested.
