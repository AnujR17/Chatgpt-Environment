# S2 wireframe review

Reviewed 10 October 2026. The user asked for an explanation of the project direction, the supplied wireframe's intricacies, missing elements and our contribution. This is a review, not approval of a redesign. The original HTML is preserved under `../source/incoming/2026-10-10/`.

## Direction

The project should produce and evaluate an evidence-and-policy review workflow for human agents. Its intended value is reduced investigation effort while retaining deliberate human judgement and visibility of all parties. The four-column shell serves that workflow; fitting every inventory item on screen is not itself evidence of usability.

S2 is especially useful because it combines a wrong-item claim, absent delivery photo, later customer photo and prior refunds. It tests whether agents reason from current-case evidence or substitute a judgement about the customer. The wireframe introduces a 1440×900 frame, 56px queue, 372px case column, 572px chat and remaining insights column; visual styling is intentionally provisional. These choices are reported by the new source, not independently approved in this chat.

## How the parts work

- Queue manages parallel chats. Define whether timing means waiting for an agent, time since a customer's last message, or total time open. A 56px rail saves width but hides labels and relies on learnt codes.
- Case information supplies the order, timeline, evidence provenance, party history and restaurant permission.
- Chat remains available while investigation continues; the bot handover should prevent repeated questions and contradicting prior offers.
- Conditions state checks against source-labelled rules. The agent then determines an outcome, amount, explanation and whether review is needed.
- Similar cases are optional precedents, not prescriptions. The log should preserve the reasons and observations relevant to an actual decision.

## Priority 1: define decision semantics

### Evidence presence and evidence supporting the claim are different

P5 is labelled Met because a customer photo exists. That is consistent with the frozen availability condition, but agents may read it as evidence that the wrong-item claim is established. Add a distinct agent assessment with recorded rationale/link: not yet assessed, supports the item discrepancy, insufficient to assess, or conflicting evidence. These are proposals for human-entered observations, not automatic authenticity scores. Keep the availability fact separate.

A photo submitted 40 minutes after the complaint was submitted four minutes after the agent requested it in this chat (21:05 request, 21:09 photo). The first interval alone is not evidence of suspicious delay. Capture time is not recorded. Show relevant event context and avoid treating submission delay or unknown capture time as dishonesty.

### Outcome, amount and authority need a coherent sequence

The affected curry costs ₹240; total order value is ₹350. P7 explicitly states a range up to ₹240. Full refund therefore needs a defined scope: affected item or entire order. The wireframe permits selecting both curry and naan, giving ₹350, while P7 and P8 remain Unknown and say the amount is not chosen. Within the current proposed rules, ₹240 would satisfy the stated item range and ₹500 authority limit; ₹350 exceeds the displayed P7 range but remains below P8. That difference must be visible rather than collapsing into a general approval state.

Distinguish draft selection from submission. Before final action, show the selected outcome, affected items/amount, policy version, evidence links, reason and any required review. Human choice does not imply every choice is within the agent's authority. Define an exception/review route without silently rejecting every unmet condition. Denial requires an explanation, and sending to review requires a handoff, not merely a button click.

### Fault needs an unresolved state

Customer/Restaurant/Rider/Swiggy are the only fault choices. Default liability is not a finding about the incident. Provide a way to record responsibility not established, or shared responsibility if the scenario model supports it. Permission to refund also does not itself mean the restaurant admits a mix-up. Record permission scope, permitted amount/items and the actor/time rather than treating a bare Given status as universal authorization.

## Priority 2: protect neutral interpretation

History is repeated in the status strip beside review triggers. This increases its decision salience despite the context label. Prefer a distinct context section; test collapsed versus always-visible summaries rather than assuming hiding or displaying history solves bias. Customer refunds, restaurant complaints and rider reports are different events; identical typography and denominators alone do not make them comparable or prove fault. Define counted events, resolved/unsubstantiated statuses, date windows and a common older-history interaction across parties.

The similar-cases drawer matches only wrong item, P3 and P5/evidence presence in its visible description. The frozen specification calls for matching P3–P8 and evidence patterns. One precedent says the restaurant confirmed the mix-up, which is a material extra fact not shown for this case. Show material differences alongside matches and verify the selection rules before presenting precedent outcomes. The drawer covers most of the conditions/decision column; evaluate whether agents lose the current-case context while comparing.

## Priority 3: complete the operating states

Define the state transitions before visual polish: photo requested/pending/submitted/assessed; permission not requested/pending/given/refused/no reply; draft decision/review/submission/failure; reviewer handoff and returning to the chat. Simulate incoming messages and switching chats without losing each case's draft, evidence links or permission state. Specify each clock's start/reset event and distinguish acknowledgement from resolution in the regulatory timeline. Only simulate these states in the concept; this review does not request live customer messaging or refund integrations.

## Browser checks and limits

The HTML was loaded into headless Chromium using Playwright. External requests, including Google Fonts, were blocked; measurements use fallback fonts. No JavaScript page errors occurred. See the accompanying runtime JSON and preview PNG.

- Base column widths measured 56 / 372 / 572 / 438px (the source rounds the last to 440; borders consume the difference). The case column had 901px content in 854px available, approximately 47px overflow with fallback fonts. The source reports approximately 66px with its own test conditions. Neither is a production fit claim.
- P5 highlights the customer photo, matching chat photo and missing delivery-photo placeholder. P3 has no data-link target. Evidence-to-condition linking is displayed but not implemented as a persistent action.
- Selecting both refund items produces ₹350 but does not recompute P7/P8.
- Show older changes its label and logs a customer-history opening but reveals no older records. Restaurant/rider Dates controls do not implement equivalent histories.
- The reply composer is a non-editable div; Send and most final-outcome buttons have no handling. This is an interactive low-fidelity wireframe, not a complete decision prototype.
- Queue items are nonfocusable divs. Hover reveals full labels, but the focus-within alternative has no focusable queue control. The advertised keyboard shortcut is not implemented.
- Similar-case drawer has no dialog role or focus-management implementation. These controls need accessible behavior before keyboard-based usability testing.

These are boundaries of what can be tested now, not reasons to discard a low-fidelity proposal. Density should also be evaluated at 1366×768 and 200% zoom; scaling the fixed frame to fit can make dense text unreadable. Revised actual-font measurements are necessary before concluding exact fit.

## Next deliverable and evaluation

Prepare an S2 decision/state map, then a revised low-fidelity interaction pass that makes evidence assessment, amount/authority checks, neutral context and final confirmation usable. Extend it to S3 conflicting records and S5 permission stalled before polishing, then S1 and remaining scenarios.

Compare a version with the same case information arranged without the traceability interactions against the revised prototype. Use consistent tasks and counterbalanced order where feasible. Measure decision time, lookup count, missed checks, explanation quality and effort; observe whether history and precedent change reasoning. Fictional cases can have specified facts and policy-consistent routes, but ambiguous scenarios need acceptable reasoned paths rather than a single asserted true outcome. Report participant experience and any proxy-testing limitations. No improvement has been demonstrated by this review.
