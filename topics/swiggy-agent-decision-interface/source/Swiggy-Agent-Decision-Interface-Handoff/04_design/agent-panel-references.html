2. How peers carry a consumer brand into internal tools

CompanyWhat they publishedWhat it means for us
Uber · Base WebBuilt because Uber has "hundreds of internal web applications used by developers, product managers, and operations teams". One entry point to theme "all design tokens, including colors, sizing, and typography".Internal tools share the consumer brand's foundations (type, spacing, tokens) but get a theme suited to dense work.
DoorDash · PrismOne design language with presets for Default, Caviar and Merchant. Teams building tools "used by support agents" previously had to build custom UI elements.Support tools need a deliberate theme of their own, or they drift. The same component can recolour per product.
Zomato · design systemGreen buttons for actions. Red "limited to deletion and cancellation actions". Three greys, a palette of 10 colours in total. Consumer examples only.Keep the palette small, and give each colour one job.
Shopify · adminMostly neutral text. Orange means "in-progress or pending", and red only means blocked or an error. "Avoid relying on color alone."Status colours need fixed roles. Orange already means "pending" in operational UIs, which matters for Swiggy's orange.

Sources: Uber: Introducing Base Web · DoorDash: Design language system theming · Developing the Zomato design system · Shopify: App visual design

Pattern across all four: internal tools keep the brand's foundations (type, corner radius, spacing), keep large surfaces neutral for long shifts, limit the brand colour to identity and actions, and give status colours fixed roles that never rely on colour alone.

3. Revised layout: chat in the centre, queue on the left

QYour queue: the agent's own pending chats. Issue type and wait time only. Sorted by wait time, with no priority score or customer label.
M1–M4Case information, moved to the left so it sits beside the queue.
C1–C3Chat in the centre, the most comfortable reading position.
I1–I4Insights and decision on the right. The four decision buttons carry equal weight.

Trade-off with chat in the centre: a policy condition (right) and its evidence (left) now sit on opposite sides of the chat (dashed orange line). The fix: selecting a condition highlights its evidence on the left, and the condition card also shows a small thumbnail of the linked evidence, so the agent doesn't have to look back and forth. Test this at Stage 9.

4. Two visual-kit options, both built on Swiggy's brand values

Option 1: Orange for actions

Recommended

Selected case / condition uses Salt with an orange edge

Insight: no delivery photo on record
  • Swiggy Orange marks identity, the primary action (Send) and the current selection only.
  • Orange and amber are never used for status, so orange can't be misread as "pending" or "warning".
  • Surfaces are warm neutrals. Salt marks selection.
  • Decision buttons stay neutral and equal, so none of them reads as the suggested answer.

Type: Gilroy (official), stand-in for the prototype Outfit · display stand-in Plus Jakarta Sans for body text: higher x-height and clearer numbers at 13–15px over long shifts. IBM Plex Mono · 19:42 · ₹180 · 3 / 7

Gilroy is a licensed font that isn't on Google Fonts, so the coded prototype uses stand-ins with a similar geometric shape. The Figma mockup can use Gilroy itself if you have the licence. The brand book's "Gilroy Light for body" is meant for print; at screen sizes in a dense tool it's too thin, so body text moves to Regular or Medium.

Status colours (same in both options)
✓ Met✕ Not met? Unknown

Green and red follow Shopify's roles: red only means blocked or failed. "Unknown" is neutral grey, because a missing fact is a gap to fill, not a warning about the customer. Every chip has an icon and a word.
